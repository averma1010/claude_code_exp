I've been thinking a lot about simplicity lately. In web design, software, and life in general, we tend to overcomplicate things.

## The Tyranny of Features

Every product wants to add more features. Every website wants more animations, more interactivity, more "engagement." But at what cost?

When you visit a website, what do you actually want? Usually, you want information, and you want it quickly. You don't want to wait for animations to finish. You don't want pop-ups asking you to subscribe before you've even read a single word.

## What Makes Good Design?

Good design is invisible. It gets out of the way and lets the content shine. Look at the best-designed newspapers and magazines—they prioritize typography, hierarchy, and whitespace. They understand that their job is to present information clearly, not to show off.

## Digital Minimalism

This philosophy extends beyond design. In software, the best tools are often the simplest ones. They do one thing well rather than trying to be everything to everyone.

My favorite tools:
- Simple text editors over bloated IDEs (when appropriate)
- Static sites over complex CMSs
- Plain text files over proprietary formats
- Command-line tools over GUIs (for many tasks)

## The Cost of Complexity

Complexity has real costs:
- Slower load times
- More bugs
- Harder maintenance
- Cognitive overload for users
- Technical debt

Every feature you add is a feature you have to maintain. Every animation is code that can break. Every database query is a point of failure.

## Embracing Simplicity

This website is my attempt to embrace simplicity. No JavaScript frameworks (except Next.js for the basics), no complex state management, no animations. Just content, cleanly presented.

And you know what? It feels great.
